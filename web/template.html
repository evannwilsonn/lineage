      A caveat about the judge's past. The backbone was pretrained on ImageNet, and Stanford Dogs was built from ImageNet's dog photos: all 120 breeds are ImageNet classes, and many of these test photos were probably in the backbone's training set. Without any training, ImageNet's own classifier already gets {{imagenet}} of them. So these scores say how well the head reads the backbone, not how well it would do on dog photos the backbone has never seen. Expect lower on your own photos.

          Rules of the show

          How a photo gets from your device to a rosette.

          1. The photo is framed

            The page shrinks it in a canvas, then Swift code compiled to WebAssembly turns {{framing}} into the network's input. The same Swift prepared the training photos.

          2. The backbone looks

            A pretrained MobileNetV4 (timm, Apache 2.0) runs in ONNX Runtime Web and boils the photo down to 1,280 numbers. Training ran the same file from Swift through ONNX Runtime's C API.

          3. The head judges

            The head turns those numbers into 120 probabilities. It was trained from scratch in Swift: my own gradients, mini-batches, weight decay and early stopping. Its forward pass is the WebAssembly too.

          4. Rosettes are pinned

            The three most likely breeds get rosettes, the next two a highly commended ribbon. A photo of something that isn't one of the 120 breeds still gets awards, so take low numbers as the judge being unsure.
